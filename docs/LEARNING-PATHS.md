# Learning Paths

Your Next Skill organizes learning by progression so users can focus on the capability that is most useful next.

## Lessons for Everyone

Foundational AI literacy is relevant at every skill level.

| Lesson | Focus |
|---|---|
| Safe AI Use | Using AI with appropriate boundaries and judgment |
| Responsible AI | Understanding responsible adoption and use |
| AI Ethics & Bias | Recognizing ethical considerations and potential bias |
| AI Privacy, Security & Data Awareness | Making better decisions about what information belongs in AI systems |
| Verifying AI Information | Evaluating outputs rather than automatically trusting them |
| AI & the Environment | Understanding the environmental considerations surrounding AI |

## Beginner

**Goal:** Build confidence and establish useful AI habits.

Topics include connectors, Projects basics, managing AI usage, prompt packs, and a Beginner Capstone.

## Intermediate

**Goal:** Move from isolated AI usage toward repeatable solutions.

Topics include problem framing, building a first custom GPT, tool stacking, digital twins, document and deck creation, and brand/templates.

## Advanced

**Goal:** Understand and build more sophisticated AI-enabled systems.

Topics include automation, APIs, data, agents, workflow design, and testing/evaluation.

## Applied AI & Leadership

**Goal:** Connect AI capability to organizational adoption.

This path focuses on AI strategy and enablement for teams, including workforce adoption, responsible implementation, workflow transformation, and translating AI capability into business use.

## Learning Philosophy

The paths are not intended to imply that everyone needs to become advanced in every area. A useful AI skill profile depends on the person's role, responsibilities, goals, and the problems they need to solve.

The central question remains: **What should you learn next?**
